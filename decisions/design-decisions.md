# Design decisions

This page describes product-facing choices visible in retained records. Most entries are reconstructed from the evolving interface, source documentation and creator-provided history; they are not presented as dated meeting minutes.

## Describe behavior, not a person's mind

Lizard's language moved away from treating activity as proof of focus. Quiet periods can include reading or thinking; frequent interaction can be unrelated to the intended work. The product therefore presents limited signals and estimates for a person to interpret, not a diagnosis or validated attention score.

**Evidence:** current product language, the tracking methodology and the retained prototype-to-current-product record. **Unknown:** when this framing was first chosen, what alternatives were formally discussed, and whether any original decision record exists.

## Let the person choose session context

Work, Reading and Video describe the context a person selects. The category is not an automated classification of what the person is doing or thinking. Different contexts may make different session summaries useful, but none establishes attention, comprehension or learning.

**Evidence:** current user-facing session choices and the product records. **Unknown:** the full design discussion and exact introduction dates for each category.

## Show a session's shape

The product grew beyond a timer toward a timeline, completed-session history and simple patterns. This makes it possible to review events and summaries rather than relying on a single duration or score. Pattern labels remain exploratory and depend on the records available; they are not statistically validated conclusions.

**Evidence:** the current product experience and retained development/QA records. **Unknown:** the exact first release of each view and whether earlier pattern wording was evaluated with users.

## Keep the companion optional

Lizzy adds a character to the session experience but is optional. Its presence should not be read as a measurement or interpretation of a person's mental state.

**Evidence:** current user-facing enablement and product documentation. **Unknown:** the original design exploration and introduction date.

## Keep claims bounded

Screenshots, tests and Store acceptance each show different things. A screenshot documents a particular interface; a controlled test documents a tested case; publication does not prove every browser scenario or a scientific claim. Public-facing wording should preserve those distinctions.

**Evidence:** retained browser QA notes include unresolved zero-playback observations; the current product disclaimers distinguish signals from attention. **Unknown:** the complete final reports and screenshots for some browser runs were not retained.
