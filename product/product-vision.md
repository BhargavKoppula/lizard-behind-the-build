# Product vision

## Purpose

Lizard is an experiment in helping people review how their browser-based sessions unfolded. The product makes selected session signals more visible so a person can reflect on them in context.

It is not designed to diagnose attention, distraction, productivity, comprehension or learning. It is not a scientifically validated attention detector.

## The problem

A timer tells someone how much time passed, but not what the session looked like. Yet a stream of mouse or keyboard events is also an incomplete description: quiet reading may involve no input, while frequent activity may have little to do with the intended task.

Lizard explores a middle ground: show a cautious, understandable record of a session without presenting it as a measurement of a person's mind.

## Intended audience

The current product is intended for people curious about their own computer-based work, reading or video routines. Students, independent workers and digital-wellbeing experimenters may find the idea interesting, but those audiences have not been established by a formal user study.

## Principles

1. Describe signals; do not diagnose people.
2. Make uncertainty and missing observations visible.
3. Treat quiet periods as ambiguous, not as proof of distraction.
4. Keep the person in control of starting and reviewing a session.
5. Prefer an honest limitation to a confident but unsupported metric.
6. Treat privacy as a product constraint, not a marketing adjective.

## Long-term possibility

The longer-term question is whether transparent, privacy-conscious summaries can help people notice patterns they find useful. That would require a clear evaluation plan and appropriate evidence. Until then, Lizard remains an evolving experimental product—not a validated method for measuring attention or productivity.
