# 1. The original idea

Lizard began as an experiment in making computer-session time and activity easier to notice. Its earliest concept was a focus-session timer: choose a duration, start work or reading, and see a summary afterward.

The question at the centre of the project is:

> What if we could understand not just how long we spend online, but how we actually spend that time?

That question describes the project's direction, not a documented personal origin story. The available records do not establish a single event, meeting or precise day that caused the idea. Rather than invent one, this journal starts with what the early product concept shows.

The first concept explored preset and custom session lengths, active and reading modes, interaction and quiet periods, history and summaries. Some early mockups also displayed streaks, points and achievements. A screenshot is evidence of a design proposal; it does not prove that every element shipped or that its measurements were meaningful.

Lizard's purpose changed as the project encountered a basic limitation: a computer can observe some interactions, but it cannot infer attention from them. That realization became more important than the timer itself. See [the experiments and their lessons](03-learning-from-experiments.md) and the [tracking philosophy](../product/tracking-philosophy.md).
