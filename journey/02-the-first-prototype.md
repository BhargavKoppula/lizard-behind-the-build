# 2. The first prototype

The first Lizard concept was recognisably a timer, but its ambition was already broader than a clock. The prototype direction included ways to choose a session length, distinguish active work from reading, notice long quiet periods and look back at a session.

A retained 2025 prototype image shows a timer and a dashboard-like summary. It also displays weekly statistics, points, streaks and achievements. Those visuals are useful historical evidence of what the design explored; they are not proof that each feature was implemented, released or validated.

Early extension snapshots followed the Chrome extension model and carried early version labels. The surviving project record is incomplete, so this public journal does not claim that the prototype image corresponds to a particular store release or that all early calculations behaved as drawn.

The first prototype matters because it captures the initial instinct: make invisible computer time visible. The next challenge was deciding what “activity” could responsibly mean. That question led away from treating a colourful score as a measurement of focus.
